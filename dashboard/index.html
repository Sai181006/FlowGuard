THE PROJECT, WITHOUT THE MYSTERY
Small rules.
Visible decisions.
FlowGuard is a controlled demonstration of a programmable network. It measures traffic, makes a decision, and changes the switch's behavior—while keeping the evidence visible.
CONTROL PLANE = THE DECISION
DATA PLANE = THE PACKET JOURNEY
THE EXACT ALGORITHM
Count. Compare.
Wait. Decide.
  1. Calculate a rate.(Current packet count − previous count) ÷ elapsed seconds.
  2. Check the victim.More than 100 packets per second for the protected UDP service.
  3. Check the sources.At least two unprotected sources each exceed 30 packets per second.
  4. Require persistence.Two consecutive high windows. A brief burst can pass without an alert.
  5. Install precise rules.Priority 200 drops target an offender, the victim, and UDP port 9000; they expire after 10 seconds.
WHAT WAS STRENGTHENED
A sharper boundary.
A more honest demo.
  • 01
    Bind identities to physical ingress ports.
    A host cannot claim the protected client's MAC/IP from a different switch port.
  • 02
    Preserve ARP sender validation.
    Learned rules keep the same sender-address checks as the controller.
  • 03
    Close the uncounted broadcast path.
    Unicast IP traffic cannot bypass monitored flows using a broadcast Ethernet destination.
  • 04
    Align observation and enforcement.
    Only the protected UDP service enters detection and its matching mitigation rules.
WHERE THE CONTRIBUTION LIES
Integration is the contribution.
Clarity is the advantage.
The threshold detector and SDN pattern are established ideas. This project's contribution is a reproducible, inspectable implementation: source attribution, temporary rules, recovery, raw evidence, regression checks, and an understandable presentation. It does not claim a new DDoS detection algorithm.

Know the boundaries before the viva.
A single attacker, low-rate floods, spoofing outside the fixed topology, and upstream link saturation need additional defenses. Protected identities are a lab policy, not a general reputation system. The recorded off/on trials both returned 80/80 client replies; filtering was shown, improved availability under saturation was not measured.

YOUR FIVE-MINUTE DEMO
Tell the story.
Let the packets explain.
  1. Start without mitigation.
    Choose the coordinated flood. Turn mitigation OFF and play. Follow the rising server load and the packet desk.
  2. Replay with mitigation.
    Turn it ON at the same replay time. The Python detector waits for two high windows; then modeled rules stop offending traffic at the switch.
  3. Challenge the detector.
    Show a brief burst, then a second wave. Try one noisy source to explain an honest limitation. Under “Tune the model”, switch the detector (try CUSUM on the pulsing flood), choose rate-limit or graduated responses, enable back-off for the repeat offender, or add a bottleneck queue.
  4. Show the evidence.
    Switch to the recorded experiment. These are the original lab's real logs and victim measurements, with the original 80/80 client replies in both modes.
  5. Connect it to the world.
    Open the real incident, then run the live UDP check. Close by explaining what each demonstration actually proves.
